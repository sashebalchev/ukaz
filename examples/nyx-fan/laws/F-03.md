# F-03 A broken temperature reading can never keep the fan off while the machine is hot

**Law.** If the fan program cannot read the temperature, it must not treat that as "cool". A failed reading may never leave the fan stopped on a hot machine.

**Why.** Today a failed reading counts as 50 degrees, and 50 degrees means the fan is off. Idle sits at 50 to 52 degrees, so the program cannot tell "failed" from "idle". If the reading tool ever breaks, the fan would stay off whatever the real temperature is.

**Status.** CANNOT BE MET TODAY. The fan program (fw-fanctrl 1.0.4) does exactly what this law forbids. It is written in docs/FANS.md ("A failed reading counts as 50 °C, which is fan off"). Two safety nets reduce the danger but do not make it impossible: a start-up self-test (the program does not start if the reading tool prints no real temperatures) and the watchdog fan-guard (it takes the fan back if the hottest sensor is 82 degrees or more while the fan is under 2000 rpm for six looks in a row). Past that, the processor throttles itself at 100 degrees.

**Your options.** (1) Fix it: change or replace the fan program so a failed reading means "full speed" or "hand the fan back to the laptop's own control". (2) Keep it and sign an exception that names this gap. (3) Drop the law. Until you choose, this card stays unapproved, and nothing builds against it.

**Checked by.** Nothing. This is words only, on purpose: a law that cannot be met is shown honestly instead of being dressed up as checked.
