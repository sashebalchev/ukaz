# F-01 The fan is quiet when the laptop is cool and works when it is hot

**Law.** The fan is off up to 55 degrees. It is never louder than 30 percent up to 76 degrees. From 62 degrees it runs at 30 percent or more, from 80 degrees at 38 percent or more, and from 90 degrees at full speed. A hotter laptop never gets a slower fan. This holds for every temperature, and for the table that is in your configuration today.

**Why.** Your own tuning: 30 percent is about 3000 rpm and cannot be heard, 33 percent can. The fan stays off where the laptop sits at idle, and it never lets the laptop get hot with the fan slow.

**Edges.** Temperatures are whole degrees. Between two points of the table the speed is a straight line, rounded down: 56 degrees is 4 percent, not 0, so the fan is already on just above 55. Above the last point the speed stays where it is.

**Blocked.** A fan that is on at 50 degrees. A fan louder than 30 percent at 70 degrees. A fan under 30 percent at 65 degrees, under 38 percent at 82, or not at full speed at 92. A fan that gets slower as it gets hotter. A table with a speed above 100 percent, temperatures that do not rise, or no points at all.

**Allowed.** The table that is in your configuration now: off to 55, 30 percent from 62 to 76, then a ramp to 38 at 80, 52 at 84, 72 at 87 and 100 at 90.

**Open question for you.** The law says nothing about how loud the fan may be between 77 and 89 degrees. A table that jumps from 30 percent straight to 100 percent at 77 degrees would pass. Do you want an upper limit there too, for example "no louder than 55 percent below 84 degrees"? Say so and the law gets a rule for it before you sign.

**Limits.** It assumes the fan program draws straight lines between the points, as its manual says, and that the laptop's fan follows what the program writes. Neither is checked here. It says nothing about timing (the 30 second averaging) or about a failed reading: that is F-03, and it cannot be met today.

**Checked by.** Today (before you sign): the statement compiles, 12 wrong tables are each refuted with a proof, and all 23 example rows agree with the statement's own definitions. After you sign, a proof that the table in your configuration keeps every law is written and checked for every temperature, not a list of tried ones. The gate also checks that the table in the law is exactly the table your configuration (the main branch of nixos-config) says now: if the fan curve is changed there, this law fails until you read it and sign it again. The solution is a proof about the table, so there is no fan code to write, only the proof.
